Same flat in Italy. Same €300,000 price. €17,080 or €38,080 on top of it.

The difference is one declaration in the deed.

We ran the same flat through Nesso's buying-costs calculator twice. Private seller, agency fee of 3%.

As your first home, the extra is €17,080. Registration tax at 2% is €6,000. The agency fee with VAT is €10,980. Two fixed taxes add €100.

As a second home, it's €38,080. The registration tax jumps from 2% to 9%, and that one change is €21,000.

The declaration is called prima casa, and it comes with conditions. You move your residence to that comune within 18 months, or you work there. You own no other home in the same comune. You haven't used the benefit on another home anywhere in Italy. Sell within five years and you have a year to buy another main home, or the tax difference is due.

If you're relocating for work, that "or you work there" matters more than most people expect.

Neither figure is final. Buying from a private person, the tax is usually worked out on the cadastral value instead of the price, and that is often lower. The notary and the bank come on top of both.
