Article after article says Venice capped tourist rentals in October 2024.

When we checked in September, the city council still hadn't voted on it.

We needed a straight answer for Nesso's investment map, so we went past the articles and read the city's own records.

On 24 October 2024, the city's executive approved a proposal. In the historic centre, letting a flat to tourists for more than 120 days a year would need a notice to the city. The flat would also go on a city register. That was the headline everyone repeated.

But a proposal like that has to pass the council. In November 2025 a council committee looked at it and put it off. We searched the council's registers through mid-September 2026 and found no vote. The new council, seated in June, hadn't put it on the agenda.

The rule those articles describe was still a draft.

We nearly made the same mistake ourselves. Two Italian decrees are both numbered 50, one from 2017 and one from 2022, and our first citation had mixed them up. Opening the decree itself caught it.

Ten years in litigation taught me one thing about this. A summary of a law is not the law.

If you're buying in Venice to let it out, check the council's register before you trust a headline.

And if you've met a rule everyone quotes that turned out not to exist, tell me in the comments. I'm collecting them.
