Coffee on the stove. Classical music in the background. A staged sofa.

Most sellers have heard this advice. We went looking for proof that any of it raises the price.

We collected fifty research findings on getting a home ready for viewings. Then we sorted them by one question: did the effect show up in what buyers actually paid?

Coffee didn't. Scent lifts people's mood in shops, but no study we found shows a home selling for more because of it.

Classical music didn't either. It nudges shoppers a little. No housing study we found turned it into higher offers.

Staging was the surprise. In the one experiment we found, it changed how livable a home felt to its owners. It didn't change what they said it was worth.

What did show up in prices was less charming: energy class, daylight, quiet rooms, a terrace. In Bolzano, homes in energy class A sold for over 6% more than class G. That's a link in prices, not a promise that a renovation pays for itself.

So coffee can stay. It just goes to the bottom of the list.
