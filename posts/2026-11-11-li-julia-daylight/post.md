Our first design graded a room's daylight against two numbers: 150 and 300 lux.

The obvious build was to read lux off the phone and compare.

But 150 and 300 are only a factor of two apart. Two phones nobody calibrated can disagree by about that much. The same room could pass on my phone and fail on yours.

So we changed the question.

The Nesso app now takes three readings on one phone, a minute apart: windowsill, middle of the room, back wall. It shows how much of the window's light reaches the back. Whatever the phone gets wrong, it gets wrong three times.

The error divides out.

In October we went one step further. A deep room loses more light than a shallow one, so the share stopped being a verdict.

The person in the room decides.

What I took from it: a phone is a good instrument for comparing. It is a poor one for absolute numbers.
