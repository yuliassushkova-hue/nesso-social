Short-let rules show up in prices. 📉

When 18 cities around Los Angeles limited home-sharing, Airbnb listings there fell by about half and house prices by about 2% (Koster, van Ommeren and Volkhausen, Journal of Urban Economics, 2021). In Barcelona, Airbnb activity raised rents by 1.9% and sale prices by 4.6% in the average neighbourhood, and by 7% and 17% in the busiest ones (García-López and co-authors, Journal of Urban Economics, 2020).

In Italy, three big cities now close specific strategies:
· Firenze: a flat under 28 m² can't be a short let, only a let of more than 30 days. The rule survived 19 court challenges in May 2026
· Roma: since July 2026, in the UNESCO centre, a flat made by splitting a unit, or by turning a shop or office into a home, can't take tourists
· Bologna: a 50 m² minimum for new short lets in the historic city, adopted again in July 2026 after a court struck down the first version

So Nesso reads the rule before it scores a short-let investment. Where a rule restricts it or is not verified, there is no short-let score, and the district is judged on long-let yield and resale.
